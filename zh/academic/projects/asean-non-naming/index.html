Introduction

以下为英文原稿正文。

While criticized for its lack of reaction to the Myanmar crisis in 2021, ASEAN took the unprecedented step of excluding the leadership of the military government from the summit (Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Brunei Darussalam, 2021). However, in the principal collective ASEAN statements examined here, the event was described as “developments” or “situation,” rather than a coup, while the representational restriction was not framed as a sanction or suspension (Association of Southeast Asian Nations [ASEAN], 2021).

Scholarship has characterized ASEAN as long on words but short on action, emphasizing persistent gaps between commitments and implementation (Bae, 2024). However, the anomaly here is that in the Myanmar case the configuration is reversed: action preceded categorical articulation. This proposal thus asks two research questions: Why did ASEAN exclude the Myanmar military leadership from the summit without articulating its stance on the event? Under what institutional conditions does withholding categorical attribution allow a regional organization to act without triggering the obligations attached to a named category? These questions matter because ASEAN's (non-)naming determines whether the Myanmar military leadership can be dealt with as the country's diplomatic representative, and the terms on which regional engagement proceeds with a member in the midst of domestic upheaval.

This proposal argues that, by withholding categorical attribution, ASEAN keeps its actions from becoming claimable as obligations under its normative framework. For ASEAN studies, it accounts for how a regional organization exercises power without establishing jurisdiction over the matter at hand. For the study of norms, it locates the condition on which their binding force depends: a norm constrains only once an event has been assigned to it, so that withholding attribution leaves the norm intact but inoperative. For comparative institutional analysis, it draws out two structures of institutional accountability: where a procedure exists, justification is confined to whether the event falls under the category, and the consequence follows; where no comparable rule exists, every response must be defended on its own terms.
