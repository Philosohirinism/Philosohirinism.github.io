Can Security Only Be Felt?

The episode examines the experience of security and the distance between feeling secure and securing the self.

English transcript
  1. 0:01 Previously: anxiety has no object. It grows out of ‘wanting to become.’
  2. 0:06 This time, we take a step back: where exactly is the thing that makes us feel secure?
  3. 0:15 Episode 2: Can Security Only Be ‘Felt’?
  4. 0:22 Far from home, she works late into the night. When she is exhausted, she pulls out a photograph.
  5. 0:29 Her grandmother’s kitchen. Looking at it, she feels: I am still me. I have somewhere to return to.
  6. 0:35 Pause for a moment: the scene in which you feel most secure—is it happening now, or is it a memory or an imagined scene?
  7. 0:48 She wants to make that security real. At Lunar New Year, she returns to her hometown.
  8. 0:55 The old house has been demolished. A convenience store stands there now.
  9. 0:59 The security in the photograph has nowhere to take root in reality. This is where unease begins.
  10. 1:09 There are two kinds of material that make us feel secure.
  11. 1:13 Memory: my grandmother’s kitchen, my past self.
  12. 1:16 Imagination: a year from now, I can give a speech onstage in Japanese.
  13. 1:20 One points to the past, the other to the future. They share one thing: neither is happening right now.
  14. 1:28 Berenskoetter 2014, pp. 270–272: memories and visions of the future both provide material for a subject’s identity narrative.
  15. 1:34 This series’ proposition is:
  16. 1:37 Ontological security can only be felt ‘reflexively’; it can never be realized.
  17. 1:42 People can feel secure, but they can never ‘be’ secure.
  18. 1:48 This series’ proposition
  19. 1:53 Let us draw this as a circle:
  20. 1:57 Reflexive security: memory and imagination make us feel secure.
  21. 2:02 Trying to realize it: hold on to the past, or bring the future into being.
  22. 2:06 Existential anxiety: an uncertain reality contradicts that feeling of security.
  23. 2:11 Return to memory and imagination: when realization is blocked, seek comfort in that material again.
  24. 2:18 The moment realization is blocked, we are thrown back into reality. Then we look at that photograph again. The circle starts anew.
  25. 2:26 ‘Thrown’ is borrowed from Heidegger’s account of Dasein (Being and Time, 1962 English translation, p. 174); the cycle itself is the author’s argument.
  26. 2:32 What does this have to do with resistance?
  27. 2:36 One person’s security comes from the past—from ‘who I already am.’ He wants to hold on to it.
  28. 2:41 Another’s security comes from the future—from ‘who I ought to be.’ She wants to strive for it.
  29. 2:46 Both are trying to make the security they feel real. But in reality, there is only one chair.
  30. 2:56 Next time:
  31. 2:57 Those Who Hold On, Those Who Strive: Why Are Both Anxious?
  32. 3:06 This is UjiBricks.
