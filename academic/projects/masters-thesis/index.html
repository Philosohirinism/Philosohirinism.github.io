Institutional Weakness, Persistent Centrality

Ontological Security and the Durability of ASEAN-Centred Regional Order

Why do ASEAN member states continue to sustain regional centrality despite acknowledged limits to institutional effectiveness?

Abstract

Summary based on the completed thesis.

Why do ASEAN member states continue to sustain regional centrality despite acknowledged limits to institutional effectiveness? This thesis approaches ASEAN as an institutional setting through which member states sustain their own identities as autonomous, formally equal actors. It examines how shared narratives, diplomatic routines, and restrained collective language provide ontological security without requiring a unified regional self.

Using document-based interpretive discourse analysis, the thesis reconstructs an earlier regional template and examines three episodes: the ASEAN Outlook on the Indo-Pacific in 2019, AUKUS in 2021, and the Myanmar coup and Five-Point Consensus in 2021. It finds uneven support for the proposed mechanism, ranging from weaker evidence in the first episode to stronger evidence in the Myanmar case, where acknowledged policy failure coexists with continuing procedural repair and investment in ASEAN-centred practices.

The argument is that institutional thinness can help accommodate divergent national biographies while preserving a sufficiently shared regional grammar. Ontological legitimacy offers a supplementary explanation for the durability of ASEAN centrality alongside strategic interests, institutional inertia, and reputation. The findings remain interpretive and do not establish exclusive causation.
