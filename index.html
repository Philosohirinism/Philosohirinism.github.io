    From Voice to Systemic Exit

    The framework of Weaponized Interdependence (WI) holds that hegemonic states controlling chokepoint nodes within interconnected economic networks can leverage these positions to impose strategic coercion and sustain network centrality, while target states are presumed unable to decouple due to the prohibitive costs of exit. This article challenges this assumption by deploying Hirschman's Exit, Voice, and Loyalty (EVL) framework to argue that the abuse of WI generates self-defeating outcomes once the target state recodes economic coercion as an existential security threat and initiates exit. Through process tracing of the Sino-U.S. semiconductor conflict from 2018 to the present, the article identifies three analytically distinct phases: conditional leveraging through Voice, Loyalty collapse upon threshold-crossing, and systemic Exit. Drawing on the micro-mechanism revealed across these phases, the article conceptualizes this dynamic as the Chokepoint Paradox: the aggressive deployment of WI produces precisely the systemic fragmentation the hegemon sought to prevent. The article further challenges the dyadic framing dominant in existing WI literature by demonstrating how non-state actors and third-party states within the network shape the trajectory of coercion across all three phases.
