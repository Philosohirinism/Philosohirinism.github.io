  1. 0:01 Previously: those who hold on and those who strive are both anxious.
  2. 0:07 This time, a stranger question: can order itself feel anxious too?
  3. 0:15 Episode 4: Can Order Feel Anxious Too?
  4. 0:21 When we say ‘order is anxious,’ it is easy to imagine a giant subject worrying like a person.
  5. 0:29 This series sees it differently. Order is not a giant. It is a set of arrangements: who is recognized, who is seen, who is entitled to speak, and whose concerns receive a hearing.
  6. 0:39 These arrangements depend on particular people to sustain them through repetition.
  7. 0:44 The author’s definition of ‘order’
  8. 0:50 Back to the street dance club. Where is the club’s ‘order’?
  9. 0:55 ‘That has always been the rule.’
  10. 0:57 It is not in any one person’s mind. It is in the roster the president posts, the boxes the officer ticks, and an older member’s ‘It has always been this way.’
  11. 1:06 Each repetition says: this arrangement still holds.
  12. 1:13 ‘I do not want to dance in the club’s style, and I do not accept this ranking.’
  13. 1:19 These words hurt no one and break no law. Yet the people sustaining the arrangement all stop what they are doing.
  14. 1:26 ‘What if everyone starts doing this?’
  15. 1:32 Order does not fear danger—danger has an object; it can be guarded against and blocked.
  16. 1:37 It fears refusal: power rests on mutual recognition. When people stop recognizing their assigned identities, some become ‘unmanageable.’
  17. 1:46 Author’s OSIPP draft, 2B(i): order is anxious about refusal. When people reject assigned identities, some subjects become ‘unmanageable’ (the author cites Foucault 1991, pp. 102–103, on governmentality).
  18. 1:52 So ‘order’s anxiety’ means the anxiety of those who assign and sustain these identities when the identities are refused.
  19. 2:00 This series’ proposition
  20. 2:05 After anxiety comes action: those sustaining the arrangement explain rules, screen who can go onstage, and meet to discuss possible changes.
  21. 2:13 Notice: they are responding not to ‘the new member as a person,’ but to a question: can this arrangement continue?
  22. 2:21 Pause for a moment: is there a rule around you that everyone follows, though no one can say who made it?
  23. 2:32 Calling order ‘anxious’ does not treat it as a person with a mind. It refers to the people and institutions sustaining it.
  24. 2:40 Nor does every refusal shake the order. Most are turned away with ‘It has always been this way.’

  26. 2:53 Order is an arrangement — Rosters, attendance sheets, eligibility to speak. Not a giant.
  27. 2:57 Sustained through people’s repetition — Posting rosters, ticking boxes, ‘It has always been this way.’ Each repetition says, ‘It still holds.’
  28. 3:02 Order fears refusal — ‘I do not accept this ranking.’ Danger has an object; refusal does not.
  29. 3:06 Order’s anxiety — Those sustaining it all stop what they are doing. It is the anxiety of those assigning identities.
  30. 3:15 Order has no heart.
