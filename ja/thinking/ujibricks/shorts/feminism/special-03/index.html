  1. 0:04 “But the bride price? Not a penny less.”

  9. 0:48 “Raised for over twenty years”: the cost of raising her, settled.
  10. 0:52 “A daughter married out”: belonging transfers from one family to another.
  11. 0:56 “Give us a grandson soon”: her childbearing belongs to his family.
  12. 0:59 Together, this is a transfer contract: her family gets the money; his family gets her labor, childbearing and belonging.
  13. 1:07 Gayle Rubin, “The Traffic in Women” (1975): in Lévi-Strauss's kinship systems, women are exchanged objects. The relationship forms between those exchanging her. (Paraphrase)
  14. 1:15 Second cut: this is employment. The payment buys an entitlement that can be claimed again and again.
  15. 1:21 “We paid the bride price. Of course we'll spend New Year with my family.”
  16. 1:25 “Our family paid for you. You should be considerate.”
  17. 1:30 The hierarchy need not be spoken. First it is written as a number. Every later “be considerate” collects interest.
  18. 1:38 Carole Pateman, The Sexual Contract (1988): marriage appears to be a contract between equals, but establishes male domination and female subordination. (Paraphrase)
  19. 1:45 Third cut: this is objectification.
  20. 1:50 Education adds value, age subtracts it; local household registration adds value, remarriage subtracts it. A whole person becomes a list of priced attributes.
  21. 1:57 However high the price, it still says only that she is a valuable object.
  22. 2:05 Now look at that “feminist's” checklist.
  23. 2:11 Reject every tradition that costs you. Accept every tradition that benefits you.
  24. 2:18 But they belong to the same system. Bride price pays for the daughter likened to “water poured away.” You cannot reject the bill but accept the sale proceeds.
  25. 2:29 Episode 1 asked: how radical you are depends on which relationships you seek to change.
  26. 2:37 What relationship changes when you negotiate a higher bride price? None. Only the price changes.
  27. 2:42 When you bargain over your own price, you have already accepted that you can be priced.
  28. 2:52 Let's examine four common defenses, one by one.
  29. 2:55 “Bride price gives women security.”
  30. 2:58 Security should come from income, property and law, not a buyer's one-off payment. Relying on it for “security” accepts that you are the party whose value depreciates. That is exactly what must be overturned.
  31. 3:10 “The money isn't mine. It goes to my parents.”
  32. 3:13 Then it is worse: the parents make the deal. You are not even the seller. You are what is being traded.

  34. 3:23 Demands in both directions are not equality. They are pricing in both directions.

  36. 3:30 Feminism never requires respect for oppressive customs. “Respect” and “being considerate” are this system's most useful words.

  40. 3:56 Article 5: if cohabitation was brief and bride price excessive, consider cohabitation, pregnancy and childbirth in deciding whether it must be returned.

  42. 4:16 Civil Code, Article 1042; Supreme People's Court provisions on bride price, effective February 1, 2024, Articles 5 and 6.
