Does “I Chose It” End the Discussion?

Two apparently similar choices open a discussion of voluntariness, alternatives, and different conditions of freedom.

English transcript
  1. 0:01 Last time: giving birth was treated as an IOU.
  2. 0:04 “I chose this freely.”
  3. 0:06 Does that cancel the debt?
  4. 0:12 Imagine two women making the same choice: leaving work to care for their families full-time.
  5. 0:18 “I chose this freely.”
  6. 0:21 Let's begin by believing them.
  7. 0:26 Savings: in her own name.
  8. 0:28 Her former field: she can return anytime.
  9. 0:31 Her partner: willing to take turns providing care.
  10. 0:35 If she wants to return to work tomorrow, the door is open.
  11. 0:41 Savings: in her husband's name.
  12. 0:43 Her former field: hard to return after a three-year gap.
  13. 0:47 Her partner: “Didn't you choose this?”
  14. 0:51 If she wants to return tomorrow, the door is closed.
  15. 0:57 The same choice, different degrees of freedom.
  16. 1:00 The difference is not whether she wants it, but whether she can leave.
  17. 1:07 “Choosing freely” has at least three layers:
  18. 1:10 Willingness: does she want to?
  19. 1:12 Options: what else can she choose?
  20. 1:15 Exit costs: what does changing her mind cost?
  21. 1:20 “I chose this freely” answers only the first.
  22. 1:26 Political theorist Michaele Ferguson criticizes “choice feminism” for the fear of politics behind it.
  23. 1:34 Refusing to judge any choice also accepts the existing order in which choices are made.
  24. 1:43 “Respect her choice” can mean two things.
  25. 1:46 One: I will not decide for you.
  26. 1:49 The other: you chose it; face the consequences alone.
  27. 1:54 The second sounds respectful, but shuts the door.
  28. 2:00 This does not mean they lack judgment.
  29. 2:04 Nor that we know better than she does what she “really” wants.
  30. 2:10 We are not judging the choice itself. We ask what conditions shaped it.
  31. 2:19 What if the conditions changed?
  32. 2:22 Savings: in her own name.
  33. 2:25 Her former field: no penalty for a career break.
  34. 2:29 Her partner: taking turns providing care is no favor.
  35. 2:33 She might still choose to stay home.
  36. 2:36 But then, “I chose this freely” would truly mean something.
  37. 2:42 Respecting someone's choice includes asking whether she has the means to choose otherwise.
  38. 2:48 A choice's freedom is measured by the cost of leaving, not how willingly someone enters.
  39. 2:58 Next time, we keep asking:
  40. 3:01 He loves women. Why might he still be a misogynist?
  41. 3:09 This is UjiBricks.
