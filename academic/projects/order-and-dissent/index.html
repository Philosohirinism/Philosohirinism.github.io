Political Order, Dissent, and the Politics of Recognition

Subjects may not always satisfy with assigned identity, since assigned identity concerns societal narrative instead of individual private experiences. This proposal defined them as dissent group, who aims to create a satisfied self-image in order to reject the assigned identity they felt misunderstood. The political order here is constructed, sustained and also “anxious” in subjects’ reaction towards this assigned identity. Dissent group is also anxious not in the creation or modification of their self-image, but in how to make this modified self-image recognized by others: the assigned identity is already equipped with its positionality and relationality within the order — who, when and how to interact, and in what hierarchy it is situated — a nature with which the self-image created by the dissent group is not equipped. The synthesis of this two anxiety creates the irony here, that uncertainty which could disturb order’s stable function generated inside the order, and externalized into dissent group as the “unmanageable” anxiety, while dissent group is anxious in realizing its satisfied self-image into recognition, since the image is created not by the order with pre-given position within the society. From the perspective of the order, anxiety is relieved by the actions taken by exactly those actors who created that anxiety.

Abstract

Selected from the author’s current manuscript; original wording preserved.

Subjects may not always satisfy with assigned identity, since assigned identity concerns societal narrative instead of individual private experiences. This proposal defined them as dissent group, who aims to create a satisfied self-image in order to reject the assigned identity they felt misunderstood. The political order here is constructed, sustained and also “anxious” in subjects’ reaction towards this assigned identity. Dissent group is also anxious not in the creation or modification of their self-image, but in how to make this modified self-image recognized by others: the assigned identity is already equipped with its positionality and relationality within the order — who, when and how to interact, and in what hierarchy it is situated — a nature with which the self-image created by the dissent group is not equipped. The synthesis of this two anxiety creates the irony here, that uncertainty which could disturb order’s stable function generated inside the order, and externalized into dissent group as the “unmanageable” anxiety, while dissent group is anxious in realizing its satisfied self-image into recognition, since the image is created not by the order with pre-given position within the society. From the perspective of the order, anxiety is relieved by the actions taken by exactly those actors who created that anxiety.
